# Lumora Fiction administration guide

This guide is for Administrators and Editors running a Lumora Fiction site. For installation see [INSTALLATION.md](INSTALLATION.md); for sign-in protection, spam protection and proxy settings see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Day to day

- **Dashboard.** Shows anything that needs attention, such as the installer folder still being on the server, and, for administrators, who has joined and what has changed recently.
- **Awaiting approval.** Editors and Administrators see the stories waiting for review here, the one waiting longest first (click the **Title**, **Author**, **Chapters**, **Words** or **Sent** heading to sort by it instead), and the dashboard shows how many there are. **Preview** a story to read it the way it will appear, then **Approve** it to publish it, or **Send back** with a reason the author will read; a sent-back story becomes a draft again. Taking a published story down (**Unpublish** on its form) keeps the story, its chapters and its classification, and removes it from every public list at once. Every decision is recorded in the audit log with who made it.
- **Classification.** The lists writers choose from when they describe a story: fandoms, characters, relationships, genres, tags, categories (for original fiction), ratings, archive warnings, and relationship categories. Ratings and warnings are separate lists, and one is never worked out from the other. Writers can add new fandoms, characters, relationships, and tags while they write; these can be used at once and are marked **not yet reviewed** until you approve them. You can rename a term, add other names that lead to it (aliases), merge duplicates so every story moves to the term you keep, and delete terms nothing uses. Characters and relationships can belong to a fandom, so the same name in two fandoms stays two terms. Choose which lists a story must have before it is submitted; a list with nothing on it is never required, and is left off the story form and the search filters. The short lists (ratings, warnings, categories, genres and relationship categories) have a description for each value, shown to writers beside their choice, and can be put in the order you like with **Move up** and **Move down**; **Hidden** takes a value off the choices and the public indexes while stories that already have it keep it. The ready-made starter lists (see Settings > Archive) are optional ways to fill them. Lists that your archive setting hides (for example fandoms on an original-fiction site) are kept and come back if you switch the setting back.
- **Users.** Administrators see everyone on the site here, newest first, and can search by display name, login name or email address and filter by role and status. The list and each person's page show their login name, which only Administrators see. Click a column heading (**Name**, **Email address**, **Role**, **Status** or **Joined**) to sort by it, and click it again to reverse the order; the arrow shows the column in use, and the search and filters are kept. Open a person to see their role, status, when they joined, whether their email address is confirmed, how many stories they have in each state, and a link to their author page once they have published. From there an administrator can change their role, and for an Author choose how their stories are published: follow the site setting, publish without review, or have every story reviewed. **Suspend this account** asks first, signs the person out at once, stops them signing in or writing, and can also take all their published or waiting stories out of view (kept as drafts, and the page says how many); **Reinstate** lets them back in, and a person who never confirmed their email address goes back to waiting for that when the site requires it. You cannot change your own role or suspend yourself, and the site always keeps at least one active administrator. Each change can carry a reason and is recorded in the audit log with who made it.
- **News.** Editors and Administrators write announcements for readers here: downtime, rule changes, events, new features. **Add a news post** takes a title and text in the same editors as a chapter (Visual, Markdown, HTML or plain text) and has **Save draft**, **Publish** and **Unpublish** buttons, a preview drawn by the chosen theme, and an unsaved-changes warning; deleting asks on a page of its own. **Pin to the top** keeps a post above newer ones. The list can be searched, filtered by draft or published, and sorted by clicking a column heading, and the dashboard shows how many posts there are. A post is not a story: it has no categories, tags, comments or schedule, and any Editor or Administrator can change any post. A post's address (`/news/12-its-title`) follows its title until the first time it is published and stays the same afterwards, even if it is unpublished and published again. Every change is recorded in the audit log.

## Appearance

**Themes** lists the themes installed on the site as cards with a preview picture, and lets an administrator choose the one the public side uses. **Details** opens a window with the theme's version, author, description and what it needs (it is also an ordinary section on the page when scripts are off). A theme can be added by uploading a ZIP of its folder under **Add a theme**, updated from a newer ZIP in its Details (its saved options and its place as the theme in use are kept), and deleted: tick one or more cards, choose **Delete**, and confirm; the theme in use and the built-in theme cannot be deleted, and a theme that comes with Lumora Fiction asks for a clear confirmation because the next update puts it back. A theme is program code that runs on your site, so only add themes you trust; a host that prefers to copy theme folders in by hand can switch uploading off with `'theme_uploads' => false` in `config/config.php`. Copying a theme's folder into the `content/themes/` folder of your site still works and shows up in the list at once. If the theme you chose is removed or stops working, the site keeps working with a plain built-in theme and the screen tells you, and putting the theme back restores it.

**Theme options** shows the settings the chosen theme offers, such as colours and reading width; the bundled **Storybook** theme is the default and offers the story typeface and the footer credit, while readers choose light or dark colours, text size and reading width themselves, remembered in their browser; anything left on "use the theme's own" keeps the look its designer chose. Previews of draft stories and chapters are drawn by the chosen theme, so a preview is exactly what readers will see.

## Settings

- **Settings > Archive.** Choose whether the site publishes fanfiction, original fiction, or both; whether anyone can register; what role new accounts receive; whether new accounts must confirm their email address; whether a new Author's first story waits for approval; and whether visitors must sign in to read. The recommended settings are the defaults, and the archive is open to read unless you change that. **Starter lists** adds ready-made values to a list so you do not have to type them: ratings in the AO3 style, the FanFiction.net style, a film-rating style or a minimal three-value set; archive warnings in the AO3 style or a minimal set; relationship categories (F/F, F/M, Gen, M/M, Multi, Other); and whether writers may mark a story abandoned. Each has a short description written for this site, and none is ever applied unless you choose it. Adding a list only adds what is missing, so it never removes or changes a value you renamed, hid, described or reordered, and adding it again only brings back values you deleted. If you would rather make your own values, leave a list empty and add them under Classification.
- **Settings > Front page.** How many recently published and how many recently updated stories the home page lists (8 each by default, from 0 to 30; 0 leaves that list off the page). Whether anyone can register is still set under Settings > Archive.
- **Settings > News.** The switch **Show news on the site** and how many posts the home page shows (3 by default, from 0 to 20; 0 leaves news off the home page). Switching news off hides the News page, every post address, the News link in the menu and the home page block, and keeps all the posts for when it is turned back on. The admin News screens stay available.
- **Settings > Retention.** How many days a story or chapter stays in the trash before it is deleted for good (30 by default; 0 keeps them until someone deletes them), and how many earlier versions are kept for each chapter (20 by default, from 1 to 200). A long chapter's versions take room in the database, so a lower number saves space on a small host.
- **Settings > Editor.** Choose the editor writers start with: Visual (a formatting toolbar), Markdown, HTML, or plain text. A chapter can be switched to another mode at any time without losing its formatting, and you can lock everyone to your choice. The editors are part of Lumora Fiction and need no internet connection. Text pasted from Google Docs or Word keeps its italics, bold, underline, links and lists in the Visual and Markdown editors (the Markdown editor receives the formatting as Markdown), and any HTML in a chapter gets the same treatment when it is saved. Tables are not supported, so a pasted table becomes one paragraph per cell. Pasting into the HTML or plain text box brings in only plain text, because that is all a browser hands to a text box.
- **Settings > Spam protection** and **Settings > Security** are described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Maintenance

- **Maintenance > System Information.** Shows what your server offers and how the site is set up. **Download a snapshot** produces a file you can send when asking for help. It leaves out passwords, the secret key, the database name and table prefix, and the location of folders on the server.
- **Maintenance > Updates.** Updates a site without FTP or SSH, and only ever when an Administrator asks. **Check for updates** asks GitHub for the newest release (stable releases only, or pre-releases too, and the repository can be changed for a fork); nothing is checked on a schedule. **Download and review** fetches the release package and refuses it unless it matches the SHA-256 checksum published with the release. A host that cannot reach GitHub uses **Upload a release package** instead; pasting the checksum from the release page makes the upload verifiable too. The review screen shows the version change and a list of checks: the update must be newer than what runs, the server's PHP, extensions and database server must meet what that release needs, the database must not contain changes from a newer release, the web server must be able to write to every folder the update changes, and there must be enough free disk space for the package and the backup. Every PHP file in the package is also parsed before anything is replaced. Confirming starts the update, which carries on by itself on a progress page, one step per request so a slow host's time limit is never hit: unpack and check, close the site to visitors, back up the files, back up the database, install the new files and apply the new database changes, check the new version, finish. If any step fails after the backup exists, the files and the database are restored from the backup automatically and the screen says why. If the browser is closed halfway, the Updates page offers **Resume or cancel it**; cancelling before anything is replaced just ends the run, and cancelling after puts the earlier version back. Your configuration file, uploaded files, logs and any plugin or theme the release does not ship are never changed. A bundled plugin or theme you deleted stays deleted, and one the release newly adds is installed but not turned on. A plugin or theme that ships with the release is replaced as a whole folder, so keep your own changes to one in a copy under another folder name. The page keeps a history of every update, restore and backup and the update log.
- **Maintenance > Backups.** A backup is a ZIP of the application files (code, plugins, themes, server rules) and a copy of the database, kept in `storage/backups/`. One is made automatically before every update and before every restore, and **Back up now** makes one on demand. Each backup is read back and checksummed when it is made and again before it is restored. **Download files** and **Download database** save a copy off the server; a backup holds every private draft and every password hash, so keep a downloaded one safe. **Restore** (after a confirmation page) puts the files and the database back as they were, makes a safety backup of the present state first, and loses everything written to the database since. Uploaded files and the configuration file are not part of a backup, so copy those yourself when moving hosts. Backups are never deleted automatically; **Delete** removes one.
- **Maintenance > Maintenance mode.** Shows visitors a short notice (your own message, in the site's theme, with a status that tells search engines to come back later) instead of the site. Switch it on by hand, optionally with a time it ends by itself, or schedule a period (times are in the server's time zone). Signed-in Administrators, the whole admin area, and the addresses or ranges on the allowed list still see the site; Editors and Authors work in the admin area, which is never held. An update closes the site to visitors by itself while it runs, with a plain page that needs nothing from the database.
- **Maintenance > Plugins.** Plugins add features to the site. To install one, copy its folder into the `content/plugins/` folder of your site, then open this screen and press **Turn on**. Each plugin shows its version, author and description, and a plugin that needs a newer PHP or a newer Lumora Fiction than yours says so and cannot be turned on. A plugin is run once as you turn it on, and one that fails to start is refused with the reason. If a plugin that is already on later breaks (after an edit, or a PHP upgrade), the site carries on without it, the dashboard says which plugin was skipped and why, and the reason is also written to the error log; fix or remove the plugin, or press **Turn off**. Only install plugins from people you trust, because a plugin can do anything the site itself can.
- **Dummy Content plugin.** A bundled plugin for testing, off until you turn it on. Under **Maintenance > Dummy content** it makes the number of placeholder users, fandoms, tags, stories, chapters and bookmarks you ask for, so you can try themes, lists and search at the size of a real archive, and one button removes everything it made and nothing else. It refuses to load unless `config/config.php` of that copy of the site has `'environment' => 'development'` (or `'testing'`); the installer writes `'production'`, so a live site can never run it by accident. Do not set that on a site with real readers. Its [own README](../content/plugins/dummy-content/README.md) has the details.
- **Visitor Stats plugin.** A bundled plugin that counts how often stories are read, using this site alone. Turn it on under **Maintenance > Plugins**, then tick **Count views** on its **Visitor stats** screen; until then nothing is counted. One view is counted each time a reader who is not signed in opens a story or chapter; signed-in people, previews, drafts, stories waiting for approval and robots never count. Only totals for each day are kept, with no cookies, no IP address, no browser text and only the domain of another site as a referrer. The dashboard shows today, the week, the month, all time and the most read stories; the screen adds views over time, referrers, browsers, devices and, if you import a free GeoLite2 Country CSV yourself, countries. Authors see the views of their own stories on each story's screen. Its [own README](../content/plugins/visitor-stats/README.md) has the details.
- **Maintenance > Alerts.** One place, for Administrators only, for problems with the site itself. It gathers what used to be scattered notices (debug mode left on, the installer folder still on the server, the site not served over HTTPS, a storage or uploads folder that cannot be written to, a plugin that failed to load, registration paused after a flood) and adds alerts kept from events: a run of locked sign-ins or refused registrations in an hour, and email that the server could not send. Alerts are grouped by severity (critical, warning, notice), each with a plain message, how long it has been going, a **Fix this** link to the right screen and, for the kept ones, **Dismiss**. A dismissed alert stays hidden while the problem keeps happening and comes back if it returns after a quiet week; kept alerts are removed after 30 days. Problems that are checked live clear by themselves once fixed. The admin header shows **Alerts (N)** with the number of critical alerts and warnings, the dashboard shows the most serious few, and Editors and Authors see none of it. A switch on the screen, off until you turn it on, emails critical kept alerts to the oldest active Administrator account, at most once per alert per day.

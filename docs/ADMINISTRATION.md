# Lumora Fiction administration guide

This guide is for Administrators and Editors running a Lumora Fiction site. For installation see [INSTALLATION.md](INSTALLATION.md); for sign-in protection, spam protection and proxy settings see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Day to day

- **Dashboard.** Shows anything that needs attention, such as the installer folder still being on the server, and, for administrators, who has joined and what has changed recently.
- **Awaiting approval.** Editors and Administrators see the stories waiting for review here, the one waiting longest first (click the **Title**, **Author**, **Chapters**, **Words** or **Sent** heading to sort by it instead), and the dashboard shows how many there are. **Preview** a story to read it the way it will appear, then **Approve** it to publish it, or **Send back** with a reason the author will read; a sent-back story becomes a draft again. Taking a published story down (**Unpublish** on its form) keeps the story, its chapters and its classification, and removes it from every public list at once. Every decision is recorded in the audit log with who made it.
- **Changing several at once.** The lists you work through every day have tick boxes and an action menu, so the same click is not repeated row by row. **Awaiting approval** can **Approve** several stories, or **Send back** several with one shared reason that each author sees. **News** can publish, unpublish, pin, unpin or delete the ticked posts. **Pages** can publish, unpublish, show in the menu, show in the footer, take out of both, or delete (a placement change never changes a page's address). **Users** can suspend (optionally taking their stories out of view), reinstate, change the role, change the publishing setting, or send a password reset link to the ticked accounts, with one reason for all; your own account and the last active administrator are always skipped, and deleting accounts stays one at a time. **Settings > Redirects** can delete several redirects or make them permanent or temporary. **All stories** has the same extra actions as **My stories** for the stories you may change. Each item is changed one at a time exactly as the single action would, so every item is checked and logged on its own (the **Logs** screen also shows one summary line for the whole action), an item that is refused is skipped and counted, and anything that cannot be undone asks first. One request handles at most 100 items.
- **Classification.** The lists writers choose from when they describe a story: fandoms, characters, relationships, genres, tags, categories (for original fiction), ratings, archive warnings, and relationship categories. Ratings and warnings are separate lists, and one is never worked out from the other. **The rating is always required**: every story needs exactly one before it is published or sent for approval (a draft can be saved without), and the **Required before a story is submitted** box on this screen shows it ticked and locked; the other kinds can still be switched on or off there. A new site starts with five ratings (General Audiences, Teen And Up Audiences, Mature, Explicit and Not Rated: an author who chooses Not Rated has still answered, so the information is defined for every story), and a site updated to this version that has no ratings is given the same five; a list that already exists is never touched, and a starter list chosen at install replaces them. The last rating writers can choose cannot be hidden or deleted, and a rating that stories use is merged into another instead of deleted. Stories published before ratings were required keep showing with "Not rated", are marked "No rating" in the story list (filter with **Rating: No rating**), and their author is asked for a rating on the story form. Writers can add new fandoms, characters, relationships, and tags while they write; these can be used at once and are marked **unapproved** until you approve them. Each list can be filtered to **All**, **Approved** or **Unapproved** (with the number beside each), and has a tick box on every row and a select-all box in the header: tick several terms and choose **Approve**, **Mark as unapproved**, **Merge** or **Delete** to change them in one step. **Merge** asks which of the ticked terms to keep, moves every story that uses the others to it, keeps each merged name as an alias and sends the old pages to the one you kept; it needs at least two ticked terms, never applies to **Apply to all matching**, and leaves a character or relationship alone when it belongs to a different fandom than the one you keep. When more terms match than fit on a page, tick **Apply to all matching** to act on every match, up to 500 at a time; you are asked to confirm with the exact count first. A term that stories still use is skipped when deleting, and the message says how many were skipped and why. You can rename a term, add other names that lead to it (aliases), merge duplicates so every story moves to the term you keep, and delete terms nothing uses. While you type the name of the term to keep on a term's merge screen, the existing terms and aliases of the same kind (and the same fandom) that match are offered in a list you can pick from with the mouse or the arrow keys; the name you type or pick is still checked when you save. While you type a new term's name, a warning lists the existing terms and aliases that match, with a link to each, so a duplicate is noticed before it is made (it does not stop you saving). Both need the browser's scripting and work as plain text boxes without it. Characters and relationships can belong to a fandom, so the same name in two fandoms stays two terms. A character or relationship made under the wrong fandom does not have to be deleted: its edit screen has a **Fandom** box (the fandom's name, or empty for none), and saving a different one moves the term, its aliases and every story link to that fandom (a name or alias the new fandom already uses is refused, and the message says which). A fandom's own edit screen has a **Characters** section that lists its characters with their story counts, an **Add characters** box (one name per line, up to 100: names already in the fandom, repeated, invalid or used by a character of another fandom are reported and left out, and the rest are added) and **Move an existing character here** (the name of a character that belongs to another fandom, with the same suggestions as the merge screen). Choose which lists a story must have before it is submitted; a list with nothing on it is never required, and is left off the story form and the search filters. The short lists (ratings, warnings, categories, genres and relationship categories) have a description for each value, shown to writers beside their choice, and can be put in the order you like with **Move up** and **Move down**; **Hidden** takes a value off the choices and the public indexes while stories that already have it keep it. The ready-made starter lists (see Settings > Archive) are optional ways to fill them. Lists that your archive setting hides (for example fandoms on an original-fiction site) are kept and come back if you switch the setting back. **Names of the lists** (below the required kinds) lets you call a list what your readers call it: give each kind a singular and a plural name, such as "Pairing" and "Pairings" for relationships or "Trope" and "Tropes" for tags (both or neither, up to 40 characters, no markup; clear both fields to go back to the usual words). The new words show everywhere the kind is named: the Classification screens, the story form, term and browse pages, story cards and pages, search filters, feed titles and downloads. Only the words change: web addresses (`/relationship/…`), feed addresses and saved settings keep the fixed names, and a theme still receives the fixed name as the kind's `type`.
- **Series and collections.** Authors group their own stories into ordered **series** (**Stories > Series**) and Authors and Editors gather stories into **collections** (**Stories > Collections**). A series holds one author's stories in a fixed order and shows "Part 2 of 3" on each story; a collection holds stories from anyone, put in only by their own author, with an approval queue for the curator, and can be public or private (a private one is seen only by its curator and by staff who may edit any story). Staff who may edit any story can manage any series and collection from **Show every …**. **Settings > Series and collections** switches either feature off (the screens, public pages, feeds and sitemap entries disappear, and nothing is deleted, so it all returns when switched on again) and says who may start a collection: Administrators only, Administrators and Editors, or everyone who can write stories (the default). A draft, a story waiting for approval or an unpublished story is never listed in a public series or collection, in its totals or in the "Part N of M" line, and a series with nothing public to show answers like a missing page. Deleting a story removes it from its series and collections only, deleting a series or a collection leaves its stories alone, and deleting an account removes the series and collections it made and what it offered to other collections.
- **Importing stories.** Writers can bring their own stories across from Archive of Our Own, Squidgeworld or any other site that runs the same archive software, from **Stories > Import**: they upload the HTML or EPUB download of each work (or a ZIP of HTML files), see a preview, and confirm. Nothing is fetched from the other site. Everything is written through the same rules as a story written here: the text goes through the same cleaner as chapters, every story needs a rating, a first story may wait for approval, and new fandoms, characters, relationships and tags arrive as proposals for you to review (or are left out where writers may not add to a list). **Settings > Import** switches the screens off (nothing already imported is touched) or limits them to Editors and Administrators. A rating from the other site is matched to one of yours by name or alias, then by how suitable it is for younger readers, and the writer can change the pairing; to make a rating always land on one of yours, add it as an alias of that rating under **Classification**. Archive warnings and relationship categories need a matching entry in your lists to come across, so use a starter list or add them first. Each import has a report that the writer can download and an **Undo this import**, and the choices are written to the activity log as `story.import` and `story.import_undo`. The stories are written a few at a time (about eight seconds of work per request, on a progress page that continues by itself), so a long batch stays inside a strict host's time limit, and an interrupted run can be carried on from **Stories > Import** or stopped (`story.import_cancel` in the activity log). Uploaded files are kept for up to two hours in `storage/cache/imports` until the writer confirms or cancels, and removed when the run ends; a run left for longer than that cannot write the stories still waiting, and reports them as not imported. An account deleted for privacy also loses its import reports. The details of what is read are in `docs/IMPORT-FORMAT.md`.
- **Export.** **Stories > Export** makes a ZIP of one story, of all of an author's stories, or (Administrators only) of the whole archive, optionally with the cover pictures. It holds a `manifest.json` and one JSON file per story: the details, notes and end notes, every chapter in order with its text as stored, the classification, dates, and the series and collections the story is in. Drafts are included, because the file is the author's own work. Authors can export only their own stories; Administrators can export any author's and the whole archive, and an Editor can export a single story they may manage. People appear by display name and public address only, never by login name, email address or password. The file is built for one request and removed once sent, and each export is written to the activity log without its content. A very large archive takes a while to prepare, and the export is a portable copy of the stories, not a backup of the site: use **Maintenance > Backups** for that. The format is described in `docs/EXPORT-FORMAT.md`.
- **Users.** Administrators see everyone on the site here, newest first, and can search by display name, login name or email address and filter by role and status. The list and each person's page show their login name, which only Administrators see. Click a column heading (**Name**, **Email address**, **Role**, **Status** or **Joined**) to sort by it, and click it again to reverse the order; the arrow shows the column in use, and the search and filters are kept. Open a person to see their role, status, when they joined, whether their email address is confirmed, how many stories they have in each state, and a link to their author page once they have published. From there an administrator can change their role, and for an Author choose how their stories are published: follow the site setting, publish without review, or have every story reviewed. **Suspend this account** asks first, signs the person out at once, stops them signing in or writing, and can also take all their published or waiting stories out of view (kept as drafts, and the page says how many); **Reinstate** lets them back in, and a person who never confirmed their email address goes back to waiting for that when the site requires it. You cannot change your own role or suspend yourself, and the site always keeps at least one active administrator. Each change can carry a reason and is recorded in the audit log with who made it.
- **News.** Editors and Administrators write announcements for readers here: downtime, rule changes, events, new features. **Add a news post** takes a title and text in the same editors as a chapter (Visual, Markdown, HTML or plain text) and has **Save draft**, **Publish** and **Unpublish** buttons, a preview drawn by the chosen theme, and an unsaved-changes warning; deleting asks on a page of its own. **Pin to the top** keeps a post above newer ones. The **Featured image** panel adds one picture to a post (a JPEG, PNG or WebP, checked and saved again on the site like a story cover; SVG is refused): choose a file, describe it in a short sentence for readers who cannot see it (or tick **This picture is decoration**), and the picture shows above the text, as a small picture in the News list and the home page block, and in the news feed (as an enclosure and inside the entry). **Remove this picture from the post** takes it off again. **Or choose from your uploads** lists the pictures you uploaded before, newest first and a page at a time with how many posts use each, so a picture is stored once and can be used in several posts (the description stays per post; an Administrator sees everyone's pictures, so a site-wide banner can be reused). A picture is deleted from the site when the last post that uses it lets it go or is deleted; **Delete this upload** removes one no post uses. The list can be searched, filtered by draft or published, and sorted by clicking a column heading, and the dashboard shows how many posts there are. A post is not a story: it has no categories, tags, comments or schedule, and any Editor or Administrator can change any post. A post's address (`/news/12-its-title`) follows its title until the first time it is published and stays the same afterwards, even if it is unpublished and published again. Every change is recorded in the audit log.
- **Media.** For Administrators, the pictures uploaded for news posts (news pictures only; story covers, author pictures and theme pictures keep their own screens). The top of the screen says how many pictures there are and how much disk space they take. The list shows newest first, a page at a time, each picture's preview, file name, who uploaded it, when, its size and dimensions and how many posts use it (**Unused** otherwise), and can be filtered by uploader and to **Unused only**. **Used by** (the number of posts) opens the list of posts that show the picture, each linked to its editor; the description belongs to each post and is changed there. **Upload pictures** takes one or several JPEG, PNG or WebP files (the same checks and size limit as in the news editor); a refused file is named with the reason and does not stop the others, and the uploaded pictures are owned by you. Tick pictures (or use **Delete…** on a row) to delete them: the confirmation page names the posts that still show each picture, and a picture that posts still show is kept unless you also tick **Also remove it from the posts that use it**, in which case those posts simply lose their picture. Both stored files go and each deletion is recorded in the activity log. The screen lists the pictures the site knows about and never scans the uploads folder, so a stray file there is neither shown nor deleted.
- **Pages.** Static pages such as About, FAQ, Terms of Service and the Privacy Policy. **Add a page** takes a title, an address (the last part of its web address, such as `about`; leave it empty to make it from the title), the text in the same editors as a chapter, and where the page is linked: in the main menu, in the footer links, in both or in neither, lowest **Order** number first. A page is a draft until you press **Publish**; a draft is never linked and its address answers "not found", and **Preview** shows it to you only. An address that the site already uses (such as `search` or `news`), or that another page has, is refused. Each page can carry a short description for search engines and a switch asking them not to list it. On an empty list, **Start from a draft** adds placeholder drafts for About, FAQ, Terms of Service and Privacy Policy; replace the text, which is not legal advice, before publishing. Pages are readable by everyone, even when the archive asks visitors to sign in.
- **Tags.** By default writers may add new tags as they write, and an Administrator reviews them. Under **Settings > Archive** you can switch that off: writers then pick from the tags you have added, tags already proposed stay where they are, and the tag list under Classification can be put in order by hand. Tags (and fandoms, characters and relationships) that an Administrator adds while editing a story, or edits afterwards, count as reviewed at once, so the **unapproved** list holds only writers' proposals.
- **Comments.** Signed-in readers can leave a plain-text comment under a chapter, and a story's own writer's comments carry an **Author** badge. Under **Settings > Comments** you switch comments on or off for the site, choose whether a person's first comment waits for approval (recommended) or every comment appears at once, say whether writers may turn comments off for their own stories and chapters, set how many minutes a commenter may edit their words, and set how many reports hide a comment. The **Comments** screen lists every comment whatever its state, with tabs and counts for Published, Pending, Reported, Hidden and Spam, filters for the text and dates, and single or bulk Approve, Hide, Mark as spam, Restore and Delete (deleting asks first). From a commenter's row you can hold or block their future comments. A comment the spam checks suspect is kept as spam, never dropped. Writers see and moderate only the comments under their own stories, and are told about a new comment in their notifications, and by email if they choose, on their profile page. Readers can also ask to be emailed when the writer answers a chapter they commented on, or when someone replies to their comment. A signed-in person can **Reply** to a published comment, one level deep: there is no reply to a reply. Under **Settings > Comments**, **Allow replies** (on by default) switches the reply links off without deleting anything; replies already made then show in the ordinary list. Replies are held, checked for spam, reported and moderated like any comment, show on the Comments screen with a line saying which comment they answer (and a **Replies only** filter), are hidden with the comment they answer, and are deleted with it, after a question that says how many go with it. Turning comments off hides the form and list and deletes nothing. A deleted account's comments stay under the neutral name.

## Appearance

**Themes** lists the themes installed on the site as cards with a preview picture, and lets an administrator choose the one the public side uses. **Details** opens a window with the theme's author, design link, version and folder name, and the reason when the site cannot use it (it is also an ordinary section on the page when scripts are off). A theme can be added by uploading a ZIP of its folder under **Add a theme**, updated from a newer ZIP in its Details (its saved options and its place as the theme in use are kept), and deleted: tick one or more cards, choose **Delete**, and confirm; the theme in use and the built-in theme cannot be deleted, and a theme that comes with Lumora Fiction asks for a clear confirmation because the next update puts it back. A theme is program code that runs on your site, so only add themes you trust; a host that prefers to copy theme folders in by hand can switch uploading off with `'theme_uploads' => false` in `config/config.php`. Copying a theme's folder into the `content/themes/` folder of your site still works and shows up in the list at once. If the theme you chose is removed or stops working, the site keeps working with a plain built-in theme and the screen tells you, and putting the theme back restores it.

**Preview** on a theme card shows that theme on the real public site, on every page, to you alone, before you make it the active one. A banner on every page says so and offers **Make this the active theme** and **Exit preview**; nobody else sees the change, it is never kept by a shared cache, and it ends when you sign out. A signed-in Administrator can also add `?theme=folder-name` to any public address. **Let visitors choose their own theme** (off by default) shows a small form on every public page so a visitor can pick one of the themes you tick; the active theme is always offered, and nothing is shown unless two or more are. A signed-in reader's choice follows them to any device, anyone else gets a cookie holding only the theme's folder name, and the admin, downloads and feeds always use the site's theme.

**Footer text**, under **Theme options**, is a short text printed at the bottom of every page, such as a disclaimer ("The characters belong to their owners; no profit is made."). It is plain text up to 2,000 characters (a blank line starts a new paragraph, markup is shown as typed, never run) and is saved once for the whole site, so it is kept when you switch themes, preview one or let visitors pick their own, and **Theme options** of a theme never clears it. Leave it empty to print nothing. It is part of the settings file.

**Theme options** shows the settings the chosen theme offers, such as colours and reading width; the bundled **Storybook** theme is the default, **Rosewine** and **Lavender Mist** are two more bundled themes with gradient colours, and **Classic Archive** is a fourth with compact story blurbs in the style of a traditional fandom archive; all four offer the story typeface and the footer credit, and Classic Archive also offers **Show a picture band under the site header** (off until you turn it on) with a choice of three pictures, pictures you add by FTP to `content/uploads/theme-headers/` (named `banner-something.jpg`, `.png`, `.webp` or `.svg`) and a picture you upload yourself, while readers choose light or dark colours, text size and reading width themselves, remembered in their browser; anything left on "use the theme's own" keeps the look its designer chose. Previews of draft stories and chapters are drawn by the chosen theme, so a preview is exactly what readers will see.

## Settings

- **Settings > Archive.** Choose whether the site publishes fanfiction, original fiction, or both; whether anyone can register; what role new accounts receive; whether new accounts must confirm their email address; whether a new Author's first story waits for approval; and whether visitors must sign in to read. The recommended settings are the defaults, and the archive is open to read unless you change that. **Starter lists** adds ready-made values to a list so you do not have to type them: ratings in the AO3 style, the FanFiction.net style, a film-rating style or a minimal three-value set; archive warnings in the AO3 style or a minimal set; relationship categories (F/F, F/M, Gen, M/M, Multi, Other); and whether writers may mark a story abandoned. Each has a short description written for this site, and none is ever applied unless you choose it. Adding a list only adds what is missing, so it never removes or changes a value you renamed, hid, described or reordered, and adding it again only brings back values you deleted. If you would rather make your own values, leave a list empty and add them under Classification.
- **Settings > Archive, Who can publish stories.** A site-wide rule that decides who may write stories, whatever an account's role is. **Anyone with an Author account** is the usual setup (and the default, so a site that updates changes nothing). **Only Authors an administrator has approved** stops registering from ever making an Author: new accounts are Readers, and you give the Author role under **Users** to the people who may write. **Only staff (Administrators and Editors)** suits a single-author site: Author accounts keep their role but have a Reader's abilities (they can read, keep bookmarks and comment but not write, import or edit stories, and they no longer see the admin area), and the setting says how many Author accounts and published stories this affects before you save. Stories already published stay online. The rule applies at once to everyone; nobody's role is rewritten, so switching back restores what each account could do. A Reader keeps bookmarks, reading history, follows, comments and notifications in every mode. Under the two restricted modes, **New accounts become** offers only Reader (a saved Author choice becomes Reader, and you are told), the registration form stops talking about stories, and the **Users** screen has a **Publishing** column (Can publish or Reader only) and explains when a role or publishing-rights choice has no effect. An Author who opens a writing screen directly sees a page saying publishing is switched off for the account.
- **Settings > Archive, Reading a story on one page.** Switches the **Read the whole story on one page** link and its address (`/story/…/full`) on or off, and sets the longest page in words (100,000 by default, from 5,000 to 500,000, the same default as a PDF download). A story longer than the cap shows the first chapters that fit, says so, and links to the next chapter on its own page, so no text is silently dropped. A story with one chapter never has the link. Switching it off removes the link and the address and deletes nothing. A reader's own choice to open stories as one page (on their account page) does nothing while it is off.
- **Settings > Search engines.** **Ask search engines not to index this site** adds a noindex tag to every public page, makes robots.txt say `Disallow: /` and stops publishing the sitemap. It is a request that well-behaved search engines respect, not a lock: use **Require sign-in to read** under Archive for privacy. Otherwise the site publishes a sitemap at `/sitemap.xml` (an index of parts that list public stories, the authors who have one, the classification pages people can browse, the news page and published news posts, and published pages that are not marked noindex; drafts, stories waiting for approval and unpublished stories never appear) and a robots.txt at `/robots.txt` that keeps crawlers out of the admin, account, search and download addresses and names the sitemap. The sitemap is not published while sign-in is required to read. A real robots.txt file in the web folder is answered by the web server first and wins; crawlers read robots.txt only at the top of a domain, so a site that lives in a folder needs its own file there. Every story and chapter page also names its own address as the canonical one.
- **Settings > Redirects.** Sends visitors, and search engines, from an old address to a new one. **Add a redirect** takes the old address on this site (such as `/old-page`), where it should go (a path on this site or a full `https://` address) and whether it is permanent (301) or temporary (302). A redirect is used only where the site has nothing to show, so it never hides a page that is there, and one that would send visitors round in a circle is refused. Changing a page's address, or merging a classification term into another, adds a permanent redirect from the old address by itself, marked **Automatic** in the list (story addresses never change, so they need none). Anything under `/admin` or `/account` cannot be redirected. Every change is in the audit log.
- **Settings > Export and import.** **Download the settings file** saves a small versioned JSON file with the portable settings: Archive, Security (without the proxy list and the HTTPS-only header), Privacy (without the page chosen as the policy), Comments, Downloads, Feeds, News, Front page, Editor, Retention, Search engines, Reading, Footer text and the theme (the theme in use, the themes visitors may choose and the options of the theme in use). It never holds the site address, the administrator's email, the secret key, the table prefix, the database details, spam keys or answers, or any account. **Import** reads such a file from another site and shows a before-and-after list; nothing is applied until you press **Apply these changes**. A file in a format version this site does not know, with a setting this site does not know, or with a value its own screen would refuse (or quietly change) is refused as a whole and changes nothing. A theme that is not installed here is refused, and theme options for another theme than the one in use are skipped with a note. Every change is recorded like a change made on the screen, and the import itself is one more audit entry. The file format is described in [SETTINGS-FILE.md](SETTINGS-FILE.md).
- **Settings > Front page.** How many recently published and how many recently updated stories the home page lists (8 each by default, from 0 to 30; 0 leaves that list off the page). Whether anyone can register is still set under Settings > Archive.
- **Settings > News.** The switch **Show news on the site** and how many posts the home page shows (3 by default, from 0 to 20; 0 leaves news off the home page). Switching news off hides the News page, every post address, the News link in the menu and the home page block, and keeps all the posts for when it is turned back on. The admin News screens stay available.
- **Settings > Retention.** How many days a story or chapter stays in the trash before it is deleted for good (30 by default; 0 keeps them until someone deletes them), and how many earlier versions are kept for each chapter (20 by default, from 1 to 200). A long chapter's versions take room in the database, so a lower number saves space on a small host.
- **Settings > Editor.** Choose the editor writers start with: Visual (a formatting toolbar), Markdown, HTML, or plain text. A chapter can be switched to another mode at any time without losing its formatting, and you can lock everyone to your choice. The editors are part of Lumora Fiction and need no internet connection. Text pasted from Google Docs or Word keeps its italics, bold, underline, links and lists in the Visual and Markdown editors (the Markdown editor receives the formatting as Markdown), and any HTML in a chapter gets the same treatment when it is saved. Tables are not supported, so a pasted table becomes one paragraph per cell. Pasting into the HTML or plain text box brings in only plain text, because that is all a browser hands to a text box.
- **Settings > Privacy.** Names the published page that is your privacy policy (the registration form then links to it, and themes can too) and chooses what happens to the stories of an account that is deleted: keep them, shown under a neutral name you choose (the default is "Anonymous author"), or delete them. The choice is the usual one, preselected when you delete an account from Privacy requests, where you can change it for that request; an author deleting their own account chooses for themselves. The neutral name is used for every kept story. **Anonymous install ping** is off until you tick it. When it is on, the site sends the Lumora Fiction project a random install ID, the Lumora Fiction version and the PHP version, once straight after you save, then about once a month at most, and only while an Administrator opens an admin page (a visitor's request never waits on it). It never sends the site address or name, any email address, any story or setting, or anything about visitors. The screen shows the install ID and when a ping was last tried, and **Send a test ping now** (available while the switch is on) says whether the counting server accepted one. A ping that fails is not shown anywhere and is tried again a month later. The ID belongs to the site address: a site restored on the same address keeps it, a copy or a move to another address makes a new one. The switch and the ID are never part of the exported settings file, so an import cannot turn the ping on for another site.
- **Settings > Spam protection** and **Settings > Security** are described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Maintenance

- **Maintenance > System Information.** Shows what your server offers and how the site is set up. **Download a snapshot** produces a file you can send when asking for help. It leaves out passwords, the secret key, the database name and table prefix, and the location of folders on the server. The **Install folder** row (the site's absolute path on the server, for example when you need to put a file there) is shown on the screen for Administrators and is never part of the snapshot.
- **Maintenance > Updates.** Updates a site without FTP or SSH, and only ever when an Administrator asks. The screen starts with three groups side by side: the **installed version** (with how far the database is, and the folder the site is installed in), the **update status** (a badge saying up to date, update available, not checked yet or check failed, with the channel and when the last check ran) and the **update source** (GitHub, the repository and a link to all releases). Below are two tabs, **GitHub** and **Manual Update**. **Latest release** shows the newest release's name, whether it is stable or a pre-release, its date, the release notes as formatted text in a box that scrolls, **View release notes on GitHub** and **Download release ZIP**; a release that is not newer than the one running has no download button, because updates only go forward. Below the tabs are **Database updates**, **Full backups** (the three newest backups with Download, Restore, Delete and **Back up now**; **All backups** opens the Backups screen), **System status** (a Pass or Fail table of what the server needs, run by the same checks as the review screen, so a row that fails here would stop an update there), the update settings, the history and a short **About updates** list. The page opens on GitHub, or on Manual Update when the last check could not reach GitHub (the reason is shown), and the address `?tab=github` or `?tab=manual` opens a given tab. **Check for updates** asks GitHub for the newest release (stable releases only, or pre-releases too, and the repository can be changed for a fork) with one plain request that sends nothing about your site, your members or your stories. Unless you turn on **Check for new releases automatically** in the update settings, nothing is checked on a schedule. With it on (daily or weekly), the check is made the next time an Administrator opens an admin page once one is due, never by a background job; it only looks, and a newer release then shows a mark beside **Updates** in the menu and an alert on the dashboard. A check that fails is remembered and not tried again until the next interval. An optional **GitHub token** raises the limit GitHub puts on anonymous requests, which only matters on a shared host that has asked too often; it is stored in the settings, shown only as Set or Not set, never written to a log, and sent only to GitHub's API. Leave the box empty to keep it, or tick **Remove the saved token**. **Download and review** fetches the release package and refuses it unless it matches the SHA-256 checksum published with the release. A host that cannot reach GitHub uses **Upload a release package** on the Manual Update tab instead; pasting the checksum from the release page makes the upload verifiable too. The review screen shows the version change and a list of checks: the update must be newer than what runs, the server's PHP, extensions and database server must meet what that release needs, the database must not contain changes from a newer release, the web server must be able to write to every folder the update changes, and there must be enough free disk space for the package and the backup. Every PHP file in the package is also parsed before anything is replaced. Confirming starts the update, which carries on by itself on a progress page (the same page serves backups and restores), one step per request so a slow host's time limit is never hit; the steps and the log update in place, and if the connection drops or a step takes more than two minutes the page falls back to an ordinary form and shows what happened. The steps are: unpack and check, close the site to visitors, back up the files, back up the database, install the new files and apply the new database changes, check the new version, finish. If any step fails after the backup exists, the files and the database are restored from the backup automatically and the screen says why. If the browser is closed halfway, the Updates page offers **Resume or cancel it**; cancelling before anything is replaced just ends the run, and cancelling after puts the earlier version back. Your configuration file, uploaded files, logs and any plugin or theme the release does not ship are never changed. A bundled plugin or theme you deleted stays deleted, and one the release newly adds is installed but not turned on. A plugin or theme that ships with the release is replaced as a whole folder, so keep your own changes to one in a copy under another folder name. The page keeps a history of every update, restore and backup and the update log. Version 1.0.0 has no Updates screen, so a site on it moves to 1.1.0 by uploading the new files over the old ones (keeping `config/config.php`, `storage/`, `content/uploads/` and any plugin or theme of your own), and then signing in as an Administrator and opening the admin: the database changes that came with the new files are applied by that first visit, once, under a lock, and recorded in the activity log, and the admin shows one note, once, saying how many were applied (for example "Applied 3 database changes after the new files were uploaded."). Until then the public pages of a site running the new files can fail where they need the new database changes, so open the admin straight after uploading. If a change cannot be applied the admin still opens and a critical alert says so; the error log has the details. Take a backup of the database first, because changes that ran before a failure are not undone. From 1.1.0 on, use **Upload a release package** or **Check for updates** instead.
- **Maintenance > Backups.** A backup is a ZIP of the application files (code, plugins, themes, server rules) and a copy of the database, kept in `storage/backups/`. One is made automatically before every update and before every restore, and **Back up now** makes one on demand. Each backup is read back and checksummed when it is made and again before it is restored. **Download files** and **Download database** save a copy off the server; a backup holds every private draft and every password hash, so keep a downloaded one safe. **Restore** (after a confirmation page) puts the files and the database back as they were, makes a safety backup of the present state first, and loses everything written to the database since. Uploaded files (story covers, author pictures and news pictures, all under `content/uploads/`) and the configuration file are not part of a backup, so copy those yourself when moving hosts; an update never touches `content/uploads/`. Backups are never deleted automatically; **Delete** removes one.
- **Maintenance > Maintenance mode.** Shows visitors a short notice (your own message, in the site's theme, with a status that tells search engines to come back later) instead of the site. Switch it on by hand, optionally with a time it ends by itself, or schedule a period (times are in the server's time zone). Signed-in Administrators, the whole admin area, and the addresses or ranges on the allowed list still see the site; Editors and Authors work in the admin area, which is never held. An update closes the site to visitors by itself while it runs, with a plain page that needs nothing from the database.
- **Plugins.** Plugins add features to the site. To install one, copy its folder into the `content/plugins/` folder of your site, then open this screen and press **Turn on**. Each plugin shows its version, author and description, and a plugin that needs a newer PHP or a newer Lumora Fiction than yours says so and cannot be turned on. A plugin is run once as you turn it on, and one that fails to start is refused with the reason. If a plugin that is already on later breaks (after an edit, or a PHP upgrade), the site carries on without it, the dashboard says which plugin was skipped and why, and the reason is also written to the error log; fix or remove the plugin, or press **Turn off**. Only install plugins from people you trust, because a plugin can do anything the site itself can.
- **Dummy Content plugin.** A bundled plugin for testing, off until you turn it on. Under **Maintenance > Dummy content** it makes the number of placeholder users, fandoms, tags, stories, chapters, bookmarks, comments (with replies and reports), news posts and pages you ask for, so you can try themes, lists and search at the size of a real archive, and one button removes everything it made and nothing else. It refuses to load unless `config/config.php` of that copy of the site has `'environment' => 'development'` (or `'testing'`); the installer writes `'production'`, so a live site can never run it by accident. Do not set that on a site with real readers. Its [own README](../content/plugins/dummy-content/README.md) has the details.
- **Settings > Downloads.** Readers can download a story as an EPUB, a PDF or one HTML page. **Let readers download stories** is the site-wide switch: when it is off the download menu is hidden everywhere and every download address answers "not found", and each Author's own choices are kept but have no effect until it is back on. **Formats** chooses which formats are offered; a format the server cannot make (PDF needs the PHP `dom`, `mbstring` and `iconv` extensions) is listed but not offered. **Count downloads** keeps a plain total for each story and format, with nothing about who downloaded, and **Show the number of downloads** prints those totals beside each format. Files are made when first asked for and kept under `storage/cache/downloads/`, then rebuilt only when the story, a chapter or its classification changes; deleting a story deletes its files. A PDF needs time and memory, so one is made only for stories up to 100,000 words and fewer where the host's memory limit is low; longer stories get a message that points to the other formats. A file is only ever made for a published story: a draft, a pending or unpublished story and a trashed one cannot be downloaded, whoever asks. When the archive requires sign-in to read, downloads require it too. The PDF library is described in `docs/THIRD_PARTY.md`.
- **Settings > Cache.** Public pages are sent with headers that let a browser or a cache reuse them, and a visitor who already holds the current page gets a short "not modified" reply instead of the whole page. **Send cache headers on public pages** is on by default; under **Kinds of page** you switch the front page, story and chapter pages, listings (browse, author, fandom, tag, series and collection pages), news, static pages and search results on or off one by one, and **Never cache these paths** lists addresses to leave out (one per line, `*` stands for any run of characters, the query string is ignored). **Lifetime in a shared cache** (3600 seconds by default) is how long a cache in front of the site, such as LiteSpeed, keeps a page; **Lifetime in a visitor's browser** (0 by default, so a browser checks with the site every time and a change is never hidden) cannot be longer than that. Nothing is ever cached for a signed-in person or anyone with a session, for the admin, accounts, drafts or previews, for a page that sets a cookie, for an error page, or while **Require sign-in to read** is on. When visitors may choose their own theme, or a plugin such as Visitor Stats counts story views, the affected pages are kept only by the visitor's own browser, after checking with the site, so the choice and the counts stay right. **Cache to tell about changes** is LiteSpeed Cache, detected automatically (or chosen by hand): whenever a story, chapter, comment or news post changes, the site tells LiteSpeed to forget that story's page and the listings, and anything that can change how every page looks (settings, classification, pages, themes, plugins, series, collections) empties everything; the last purges are listed on the screen. **Check what is sent** requests a page the way a signed-out visitor would, and again as a browser that already holds it, and shows the headers of both answers (this needs JavaScript). The cache settings are not part of the exported settings file, because they depend on the server.
- **Settings > Feeds.** Feeds let readers follow the archive in a feed reader. **Feeds to offer** switches each kind on or off: the whole site, each author, each fandom, each category (for original fiction), each character, each relationship, each genre, each tag, each series, each collection, each story's new chapters, each story's comments (off by default, and it also needs comments to be on in **Settings > Comments**) and the news. A kind that is off answers "not found" and the site stops pointing to it. **Also offer every feed as a JSON Feed** adds a `.json` form of every feed. **Entries in each feed** (5 to 50, default 20) keeps feeds small on a busy site, and **Put the whole text of each chapter and news post in its entry** can be unticked so entries carry only the opening and a link. A feed lists only what a visitor can already read: drafts, unpublished stories, stories waiting for approval and comments waiting for approval never appear, and authors are named by display name only. When **Require sign-in to read** is on in **Settings > Archive**, no story feed is served, because a feed reader cannot sign in; the news feed stays open, like the news page. Only the kinds your archive mode allows are listed: an original-fiction archive does not see fandoms, characters or relationships, and a fanfiction-only one does not see categories, with a line saying which are hidden; what was ticked for a hidden kind is kept and comes back if the mode changes. Series and collections are listed while **Settings > Series and collections** has them on. The addresses are `/feed`, `/feed/author/…`, `/feed/fandom/…`, `/feed/category/…`, `/feed/character/…`, `/feed/relationship/…`, `/feed/genre/…`, `/feed/tag/…`, `/feed/series/…`, `/feed/collection/…`, `/feed/story/…`, `/feed/story/…/comments` and `/feed/news`, each also with `.json` added.
- **Sweep plugin.** A bundled plugin, off until you turn it on under **Plugins**, that adds **Maintenance > Sweep**. It lists how many of each of these can be removed now: chapter versions beyond the number kept under Settings > Retention, stories and chapters left in the trash past the retention period, password-reset and email-confirmation links that have run out, accounts that never confirmed their email address and are older than a number of days you set (they are removed like a deleted account), and reviewed tags and fandoms that no story uses. Tick what you want, look at the list of what would go, then confirm: nothing is removed before that and it cannot be undone. Nothing a story still uses is ever offered. About once a week, when an Administrator opens the dashboard, it also counts what can be removed and, past the number you set, puts one note in each Administrator's notifications. See the plugin's own README.
- **Visitor Stats plugin.** A bundled plugin that counts how often stories are read, using this site alone. Turn it on under **Plugins**, then tick **Count views** on its **Visitor stats** screen; until then nothing is counted. One view is counted each time a reader who is not signed in opens a story or chapter; signed-in people, previews, drafts, stories waiting for approval and robots never count. Only totals for each day are kept, with no cookies, no IP address, no browser text and only the domain of another site as a referrer. The dashboard shows today, the week, the month, all time and the most read stories; the screen adds views over time, referrers, browsers, devices and, if you import a free GeoLite2 Country CSV yourself, countries. The country data is imported from a file you put in `storage/visitor-stats/incoming/` (or from a path you type), which is more reliable than a browser upload for a large file; it is kept as a file, never in the database, and an alert warns when it is more than six months old. Authors see the views of their own stories on each story's screen. Its [own README](../content/plugins/visitor-stats/README.md) has the details.
- **Users > Privacy requests.** For Administrators who may manage users. Type the email address an account was registered with to find it, then **Export their data (JSON)** to get a file of everything the site holds about it (account details, stories and chapters, bookmarks, reading places, follows, notifications and the activity log), never including the password or anyone else's details, or **Delete this account**. Deleting asks you to type the person's display name, removes their email address, login name, display name, password, author text and picture, bookmarks, reading history, follows and notifications, and removes the network addresses from the activity log; if the account has stories you choose on that page whether they are kept under the neutral name or deleted (the answer set under Settings > Privacy is preselected). It cannot be undone, and the last active Administrator cannot be deleted. Every export and deletion is written to the activity log by account number. Members can also download their data and delete their own account from **Your data and privacy** on their account page.
- **Alerts.** Directly under Dashboard in the menu. One place, for Administrators only, for problems with the site itself. It gathers what used to be scattered notices (debug mode left on, the installer folder still on the server, the site not served over HTTPS, a storage or uploads folder that cannot be written to, a plugin that failed to load, registration paused after a flood) and adds alerts kept from events: a run of locked sign-ins or refused registrations in an hour, and email that the server could not send. Alerts are grouped by severity (critical, warning, notice), each with a plain message, how long it has been going, a **Fix this** link to the right screen and, for the kept ones, **Dismiss**. A dismissed alert stays hidden while the problem keeps happening and comes back if it returns after a quiet week; kept alerts are removed after 30 days. Problems that are checked live clear by themselves once fixed. The admin header shows **Alerts (N)** with the number of critical alerts and warnings, the dashboard shows the most serious few, and Editors and Authors see none of it. A switch on the screen, off until you turn it on, emails critical kept alerts to the oldest active Administrator account, at most once per alert per day.

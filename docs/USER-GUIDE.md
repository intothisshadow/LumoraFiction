# Lumora Fiction user guide

This guide is for the people who use a Lumora Fiction site: readers browsing and searching, and writers publishing stories. Site administrators should also read the [administration guide](ADMINISTRATION.md).

## Accounts

Visitors have their own pages at `/account/sign-in` and `/account/register`, linked from the site's main menu. Anyone with an account, whatever their role, can sign in there.

- **Registering.** The form asks for a private login name, a public display name, an email address and a password. If the site asks for email confirmation, the new person is told to check their inbox, and the account starts working when they open the link. The page gives the same answer whether or not the address already had an account, so it cannot be used to find out who is registered. If registration is closed, the page says so and the menu link disappears.
- **Signing in and out.** Sign in with the login name or the email address. After five wrong passwords from one address, sign-in is paused for fifteen minutes, and the page says how long to wait. A person who has not yet confirmed their address is offered a new confirmation email. **Sign out** is a button on the account page.
- **Forgotten passwords.** **Forgot your password?** on the sign-in page emails a link that works once and expires after an hour. Opening the link does not use it up; only choosing a new password does. The page says nothing about whether the address has an account.
- **My account.** Signed-in people open **My account** in the menu to change their display name, email address and password. Changing the email address or the password needs the current password, and the old address is told about an email change. Changing the password signs the person out everywhere else. Authors also choose here how the chapter editor opens for them, or keep the site default; the choice is hidden while the administrator locks everyone to one mode.
- **Writer links.** Authors, Editors and Administrators find **Post New Story** and **My stories** on their **My account** page, and Editors and Administrators also find **Awaiting approval**. Readers see none of them.
- **Moving between the site and the admin.** While you are signed in as an Author, Editor or Administrator, the site's main menu shows an **Admin** link, and the admin header has a **View site** link back to the home page. Readers and visitors never see the Admin link.
- **Your data and privacy.** **Your data and privacy** on **My account** has two buttons. **Download my data** gives you a file with the details of your account, your stories, bookmarks, reading history and follows (never your password). **Delete my account** asks for your password and then removes your email address, login name, display name, bookmarks, reading history and follows for good, and signs you out. If you have stories, the page asks what happens to them, and you choose: keep them on the site under a neutral name, with nothing that identifies you, or delete them and their chapters too. If the site has a privacy policy, the registration form links to it.
- **Light and dark.** The admin area follows your device's setting, and the button in the header switches it by hand.

## Reading the archive

The home page lists the most recently published and the most recently updated stories, with the latest news in a column beside them (below them on a phone). Each story has a page at `/story/42-its-title` with its cover, summary, notes, details, tags and chapter list, and each chapter is read at `/story/42-its-title/1`, with the author's notes, previous and next links, and its place in the story. A finished story that has a single chapter shows its text on the story page itself. When you are signed in and may change a story (its author, an Editor or an Administrator), each chapter in its list also has an **Edit** link that opens the chapter in the admin; no one else sees it. Every author who has published something has a page at `/author/7-their-name`, and every fandom, character, relationship, genre, tag, category, rating, warning and relationship category has its own listing, for example `/fandom/good-omens` or `/tag/slow-burn`. Lists can be sorted by recent update, publication date, title or length, and are split into pages of twenty stories.

**News** is in the main menu, at `/news`, while news is switched on. It lists the published announcements ten to a page, pinned posts first and then the newest, and each post has its own page showing its title, date, the author's display name and the text. The latest few also appear at the top of the home page. News is open to everyone who can reach the site, including when the archive is set to require sign-in, so it can tell people about downtime or a rule change.

**Feeds** let you follow the site in a feed reader instead of visiting it. Your reader finds the site's feed from any page, a **Feed** link in the footer of every page points to it, and an author's page, a fandom, category, genre or tag page, a series, a collection, a story and its chapters, and the news each carry a link to their own feed just below the page's title ("Feed for" and the name). Or you can paste an address: `/feed` lists recently published and updated stories, and `/feed/news` lists the news. For something narrower, add `/feed` in front of the address of an author, fandom, category, character, relationship, genre, tag, series or collection page, for example `/feed/tag/slow-burn` or `/feed/author/7-their-name`, or use `/feed/story/42-its-title` to hear about one story's new chapters (some sites also offer `/feed/story/42-its-title/comments`). Add `.json` to any of them for the JSON Feed form. A feed only ever lists what you could read on the site, and a site that asks everyone to sign in offers no story feeds, because a feed reader cannot sign in.

A story that is part of a series says so under its title ("Part 2 of 3 in …") with links to the stories before and after it, and the series has a page of its own at `/series/…` listing its stories in order. **Collections**, at `/collections`, lists the public groups of stories that people on the site have gathered; a story's page names the collections it is in. Both only ever show what you could already read.

**Browse** is in the main menu, at `/browse`. It links three plain lists, **New stories**, **Recently updated stories** and **Completed stories**, and an index for each kind of classification that has something to show: fandoms, genres, categories, tags, ratings, archive warnings and relationship categories. An index lists each name with the number of stories that use it, A to Z or most used first, with a row of first letters (and `#` for names that start with a digit or a symbol) to jump to one, and is split into pages of a hundred. Only names an administrator has reviewed, and that at least one public story uses, are listed, so a new tag proposed by a writer or a tag that exists only on a draft does not appear until it qualifies (its own page still works). A kind that the archive mode switches off is not offered. Characters and relationships have no index of their own, because there are too many; a fandom's page links to its most used characters and relationships instead.

**Search** is in the main menu, at `/search`. Type words to find stories whose title, summary, author name or tags contain all of them, and open **Filters and sorting** to narrow the results by rating, archive warning, genre, category, fandom, tag, whether the story is ongoing or finished, its language, and a range of word counts; the kinds the archive mode switches off are not offered. Fandoms and tags are typed by name (an alias works too); the others are picked from a list. Results can be ordered by recent update, publication date, title or length. Every filter in use is shown above the results, and clicking it removes it. The page works without scripts, and its addresses can be bookmarked or shared. A search handles up to six words of 100 characters and shows at most the first thousand matches, split into pages of twenty, so a very broad search is better narrowed with a filter. Search pages are kept out of search engines.

Pages such as About, the FAQ, the terms and the privacy policy have addresses of their own, directly under the site address (for example `/about`), and appear in the main menu or in the links at the foot of every page when the site's administrator puts them there. Anyone can read them, even when the site asks visitors to sign in.

Only stories that are published, and chapters that are published, are ever shown. A draft, a story waiting for approval, an unpublished story or a deleted one gives the same "page not found" as an address that never existed, so nobody can tell it is there; its author and the staff who can edit it are taken to the private preview instead. A listing for a tag or fandom with no public stories, and the page of an account that has published nothing, also answer "not found". If the administrator turns on **Visitors must sign in to read**, anyone who is not signed in is sent to the sign-in page instead of seeing any story page.

## Reading a story on one page, and reader mode

A story with more than one chapter has a **Read the whole story on one page** link on its page and on each chapter page. The whole story opens on one page (at the story's address with `/full` on the end) with a list of its chapters at the top, each chapter in order with its notes and end notes, and the story's closing note after the last one. It is handy for scrolling, searching with your browser's find, or printing; each chapter starts on a new page when you print. A very long story shows the first chapters that fit, tells you so, and links to the next chapter on its own page. The chapters' comments are not repeated: each chapter says how many there are and links to them. **Back to chapter by chapter** returns to the story page.

If you always prefer the whole story on one page, choose **How to open a story: The whole story on one page** in the Reading section of your account page. A story you open from its page then opens as one page (a story with one chapter, a very long story and a link to one particular chapter are left as they are), and **Back to chapter by chapter** still gets you out for that visit without changing your choice.

**Reader mode** is a quiet layout for a chapter, and for the whole-story page: the site header and menu, the footer, the buttons, the comments and the reading controls step out of the way and the text stays with its notes and the previous and next links. Press the **Reader mode** button in the reading controls, or press the **R** key (it is ignored while you are typing in a box), to switch it on, and **Leave reader mode** (always at the top right of the page) or **R** again to switch it off; **Reading settings** brings the size, width and colour choices back while you read. Your choice is remembered in your browser, and in your account when you are signed in, so it follows you to another device. Without scripts the chapter simply shows in full.

## Bookmarks, continuing and reading preferences

Anyone signed in can press **Bookmark this story** on a story page, or **Bookmark this chapter** on a chapter page, and open **My bookmarks** from the **My account** page to see the list and remove a bookmark. The site also remembers the chapter you opened last in each story and how far down it you got, so the story page offers **Continue reading**, a chapter offers **Continue where you left off**, and **Continue reading** on your account page lists every story you have started, marks the finished ones, and lets you forget one or clear the list. **Follow this story** on a story page (or the tick box when bookmarking) adds it to the stories the site watches for you, and **Updated stories** on your account page lists the ones with a new or revised chapter since you last read them; with **Tell me when a story I'm reading has been updated** switched on in the Reading section, the story page, the account page and the Continue reading page also say so. Following is always your choice, and **Stop tracking** or **Stop tracking all** ends it without touching your bookmarks or places. Nothing is recorded for visitors who are not signed in. A story that its author later takes down disappears from your bookmarks without any message, and returns if it is published again. The **Reading** section of your account page sets colours, text size and reading width, and these follow you to any device you sign in on. The **colour button** in the header does the same in one press: each press steps through **Auto** (follow your device's light or dark setting), **Dark** and **Light**, and the button's name tells a screen reader which mode it is in and what the next press will do. On a site that lets visitors choose a theme, a small **Theme** list and button in the header changes how the whole site looks for you; it is saved on your account when you are signed in, and in a cookie that holds only the theme's name when you are not; the Storybook theme's own controls on a chapter save to your account as well. Bookmarks, places and preferences are private to your account and are deleted with the stories they point to. Each account can keep up to 500 bookmarks and follow up to 500 stories.

## Comments

Signed-in readers can leave a plain-text comment under a chapter (links are shown as text, not made clickable). Press **Reply** under a comment to answer it directly; replies sit under the comment they answer, one level deep, so there is no reply to a reply. You can edit your own comment for a short time after posting, delete it at any time (deleting a comment also deletes the replies under it, and asks first when it has any), and **Report** a comment that breaks the rules. Your first comment may wait for a moderator before it appears. On your account page you can ask to be told when someone replies to your comment.

## Writing and publishing

- **Stories.** Where Authors write. **My stories** lists your own stories, searchable and filterable by status; Editors and Administrators also get **All stories**. Click a column heading (**Title**, **Author**, **Status**, **Chapters**, **Words** or **Last changed**) to sort the list, and click it again to reverse it; the arrow shows the column in use, and the search and status filter are kept. Under each title, **Chapters** opens that story's chapter list and **Add a chapter** opens a new chapter form, so you do not have to open the story first. Tick several stories to publish, unpublish or delete them together; stories that are not yours, or that still lack the classification the administrator requires, are skipped and counted. **Add a story** opens the story form: title, summary, notes, disclaimer, language, where the story stands, an optional cover picture, and the classification lists the archive setting allows (a list marked "needed before publishing" must be filled in first). A **rating** is always one of them: every story needs exactly one before it can be published or sent for approval, and a draft can be saved without it. A story that was published before ratings were required keeps showing, with "Not rated" in place of the rating, and is marked "No rating" in your story list (you can filter for those); its form asks you to choose a rating, and the next save needs one. Fandoms, characters, relationships and tags are typed as a comma-separated list, and the entries that already exist and match what you are typing appear under the box: choose one with the arrow keys and Enter (or by clicking it) to use its exact spelling. An entry found through an alias is entered under its proper name, and one no administrator has reviewed yet is marked as new. Characters and relationships are narrowed to the fandoms you have already named. Without scripts the boxes still work as plain text.
- **Covers.** A cover is a JPEG, PNG or WebP picture of up to 2 MB (less if your host allows less), with a short description for readers who cannot see it; after you choose a picture, a frame in the shape the story page uses appears over it, and you can drag it or use the sliders to crop the picture (or leave it alone and use the whole picture); the site then resizes it and saves it again, so details in the file such as where a photo was taken are not kept. Covers need PHP's GD extension. Replacing or removing a cover, or deleting the story, removes the old picture files.
- **Publishing.** Open a story to publish it, send it for approval, unpublish it, preview it, or manage its chapters. A story waits as a draft, and an Author's first story may wait for approval, depending on the site's settings; after one of their stories has been published, later ones publish directly. If a reviewer sends a story back, it returns to a draft with the reason shown on the story form and a **Not approved** mark in the list, until you send it for approval again.
- **Chapters.** A chapter has a title, optional notes, and the editor with a live word count and separate **Save draft** and **Publish** buttons; **Move up** and **Move down** change the chapter order. A chapter is only visible to readers once both it and its story are published. **Preview** shows a draft the way it will read, to you and to staff only. Leaving a form with unsaved changes asks first, and if your session ends while you write, the page hands your text back so you can sign in again in another tab and carry on without losing it. Deleting asks on a page of its own and cannot be undone.
- **End notes.** Besides the notes shown above a chapter, a chapter has **End notes**, shown below its text and above the buttons to the next chapter (thanks, a word about the next update). A story has **End notes** too, for a closing word: they appear on the story page after the list of chapters, and after the last chapter once the story is marked Completed. Both are plain text, up to 4,000 characters, and optional; earlier versions of a chapter keep their end notes and are put back with them. They are included in downloads.
- **Author profile.** Authors, Editors and Administrators can tell readers about themselves. **About you** takes a short text, up to 10,000 characters, written in the same editors as a chapter (Visual, Markdown, HTML or plain text) with the same formatting, and **Picture** takes a JPEG, PNG or WebP picture (at least 100 pixels wide and tall, up to 2 MB, with the same optional crop as a story cover) that is resized to a 100 by 100 square. Both appear in an **About the author** section above the **Stories by** heading on your author page, and nothing is drawn until you fill something in. Everything here is public. The screen is also linked as **Author profile** under **Quick links** on your account page. Administrators can see both on a person's page under **Users** and use **Clear author information** or **Remove picture** (recorded in the activity log) if something should not be public.
- **Notifications.** Authors, Editors and Administrators have an inbox, under **Stories** and as **Notifications (N)** in the header when something is unread, for what needs them about stories. Reviewers are told when a story is waiting for review, and a writer is told when a reviewer approves their story, sends it back (the reason itself is on the story's form) or unpublishes it. Each message links to the page that deals with it, can be marked as read, and is marked as read by itself once the thing it asks for has happened. Nobody is told about what they did themselves. It is separate from **Alerts**, which are about the site and only for Administrators.
- **Trash.** **Move to the trash** on a story's page, on a chapter's page or from the bulk menu takes it out of every list and away from readers without deleting it. A story in the trash is unpublished; a chapter in the trash leaves the story, and the chapters after it move up. **Stories > Trash** lists what you have trashed (Editors and Administrators can also show everyone's), where you can **Restore** it, as a draft or unpublished and never straight to readers (a chapter comes back at the end of its story), or **Delete for good** after a confirmation page. What stays in the trash is deleted for good after the number of days the administrator has set. **Delete the story** and **Delete this chapter** still delete at once. Bookmarks and reading places that pointed at a trashed chapter are dropped.
- **Doing several at once.** Wherever a list has tick boxes you can change many things in one go: tick them (or use the box at the top of the column to tick the whole page) and choose an action. On a story's **Chapters** page you can publish, unpublish, trash or delete the ticked chapters. On **My stories** the menu also has **Set the rating**, **Set where it stands**, **Add to a series**, **Add to a collection**, **Downloads**, and adding or removing a tag, fandom or character by name; each of those asks for its choice on a page that lists the stories first, and a name that is not on the list is proposed for review exactly as on the story form. Your **Notifications** can be marked as read or deleted the same way, and on a series or a collection you run you can take several stories out at once (and approve or turn down several offered ones). A message afterwards says how many were done and how many were skipped, with the reason for the skipped ones, and the list stays on the same page, sort and filter. Anything that cannot be undone asks first, and a story, chapter or message that is not yours is never touched.
- **Series.** Under **Stories > Series** you can group your own stories into an ordered series, such as a sequel and the story before it. Give the series a title, a summary and a status of its own, then add stories (or pick the series, with an optional part number, on a story's own screen) and put them in order with **Move up** and **Move down**. A story is in one series at most, and only your own stories can be in your series. Readers see a page for the whole series, with the number of stories, chapters and words they can read, and "Part 2 of 3" with Previous and Next links on each story. A story that is still a draft, waiting for approval or unpublished is left out of the page, the counts and the numbering until readers can open it. Taking a story out, deleting a story, or deleting the series never deletes the others.
- **Collections.** A collection gathers stories in no particular order, often by several authors: an anthology, a challenge or event, a reading list. If the site lets you, start one under **Stories > Collections**, make it public or private, and choose whether others may offer stories and whether you approve each one first. Under **Collections open to your stories** you can offer one of your own stories to someone else's collection, and **What you have offered** shows where each stands and lets you take a story out again whenever you like. You can only ever add your own stories; a curator approves or turns down what is offered. A story shows in a public collection only while readers could already open it.
- **Export.** **Stories > Export** gives you a ZIP of your work: one story, or all of your stories, with their chapters in order, notes and end notes, the classification, dates, the series and collections they belong to and, if you tick it, the cover pictures. Drafts are included, because they are yours. The file never holds anyone's login name, email address or password. It is not kept on the server after you download it.
- **Suggestions while you type.** In the fandom, character, relationship and tag boxes of the story form, the existing entries that match what you are typing appear in a list: pick one with the mouse or the arrow keys and Enter to use the site's own spelling instead of proposing a near-duplicate. A name that matches an entry's alias gives that entry; characters and relationships are narrowed to the fandoms you already named, and an entry you have already added is not offered again. Typing a name nobody has used still works and is proposed for an administrator to approve (where the site allows writers to propose it).
- **Importing any HTML file.** A page saved from another site, a file exported from Word or LibreOffice as HTML, or a hand-written HTML file can be imported on the same **Stories > Import** screen. Such a file carries only its title and text, so the preview asks you for the rest: title, summary, notes, language, where the story stands, the rating (a story with no rating stays a draft), warnings and genres, the fandoms, characters, relationships and tags (typed by name, with the same rules as the story form), and, if you like, the date it was first published (dates inside the file are not trusted; without one the story is dated today). The story is always yours. Navigation, headers, footers, forms, scripts, styles and pictures are left out and counted, and fonts, colours and classes from a word processor are dropped while emphasis, headings, lists, quotes, scene-break lines, links and line breaks stay. **Cut a file into chapters** chooses one chapter per file (the default), or a new chapter at each main heading, heading or smaller heading (the heading becomes the chapter title), or at each horizontal line; you see the chapters and their word counts in the preview and can cut it again or change the order with the numbers beside them. Several general files are read as the chapters of one story in the order you gave them (a ZIP in file-name order), or as separate stories if you choose that. A file that is a download from an archive still goes to the reader that understands it, unless you tick **Read every file as a plain HTML file**. Importing the same file again warns first. Word, LibreOffice, PDF and other formats are not read directly: export them as HTML first.
- **Importing a story.** If your stories are on Archive of Our Own, Squidgeworld or another site that runs the same archive software, **Stories > Import** (or **Import a story** beside **Add a story**) brings them here. On the other site open your work, choose **Download** and then **HTML** (or **EPUB**), and do that for each work. Upload the files here (several at once, or one ZIP of HTML files) and read the preview: it shows what was found and nothing is saved yet. Every story needs a rating, so each rating in your files is paired with one of this site's ratings (a pairing is suggested, and you can change it; a rating you do not choose leaves the story a draft). Warnings and categories are paired the same way. Fandoms, characters, relationships and tags that the site already has are used, new ones are added as proposals for an administrator to review, and ones the site does not allow are left out, each marked in the preview. If several files belong to one series you can make a series of them, and a collection named in a file can be matched to one of your own collections or made for you. Choose for each story whether to save it as a draft (the safe choice, so you can read it first) or to submit it, which follows the same rules as any story you write, including approval of a first story. Tick the box saying the stories are yours, and import. The stories are then written a few at a time on a page that carries on by itself; keep it open until it finishes. If you close it or your connection drops, **Stories > Import** lists the import as not finished, and **Carry on with this import** picks up where it stopped (the uploaded files are kept for two hours; a story that was only half written is removed and written again, so you never get a broken or doubled story). **Stop here** ends it and keeps what was written. The text is cleaned the way the editor cleans it, so scripts, styles and embedded items are removed and the report says how many; your original dates are kept. Importing a work you already imported warns you first. The **report** lists everything that was imported, changed or left out and can be downloaded, and **Undo this import** on it deletes the stories the import made (including any you have changed since, which the page says). Only the HTML and EPUB downloads are read (not PDF or MOBI), nothing is fetched from the other site, and a work you cannot download cannot be imported. If the administrator has switched importing off, or limited it to staff, the screens are not there.
- **Downloads.** When the site allows it, readers can take a published story away as an EPUB (for e-readers and reading apps), a PDF or a single HTML page, from the **Download** button on the story page. The file holds a title page (title, your display name, summary, rating, warnings and tags) and every published chapter in order; your sign-in name never appears in it or in its file name. Your default for all your stories, including ones you publish later, is the **Downloads** box on your **Author profile**. For one story, the **Downloads** choice on its screen can use your default, allow downloads, or refuse them, and that choice wins over your default in both directions: you can refuse everything but allow one story, or allow everything but refuse one. Drafts, stories waiting for approval and unpublished stories can never be downloaded. A PDF is only made for stories up to 100,000 words; longer ones are offered as EPUB or HTML.
- **Copying a story.** **Make a copy** on a story's page creates a new draft with the same details and classification and every chapter as a draft. It has no cover picture, and none of the readers' bookmarks. A copy is yours, even if you copied another writer's story as an Editor or Administrator.
- **Earlier versions of a chapter.** Each time a change replaces a chapter's title, text or notes, the version it had is kept. **Earlier versions** on the chapter's page lists them with who saved them, shows any of them as it reads, and **Go back to this version** puts it back; what it replaces is kept too, so going back can be undone. Only the newest versions are kept; the administrator sets how many.

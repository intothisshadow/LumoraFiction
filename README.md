# Lumora Fiction

A lightweight, self-hosted story archive and publishing site for fanfiction, original fiction, and serialised stories. Write, share, and get lost in a story.

Lumora Fiction is in early development and has no stable release yet. This page describes what is ready to try.

## Requirements

| Requirement | Needed | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PHP | 8.2, 8.3, 8.4, or 8.5 (8.4 recommended) | Tested on all four. |
| Database | MySQL 5.6.4+ or MariaDB 10.0.5+ (MySQL 8.4+ or MariaDB 10.11+ recommended) | These are the oldest versions Lumora Fiction supports. Lumora Fiction is tested on MySQL 5.7, 8.0, and 8.4, and on MariaDB 10.4, 10.11, and 11.8, and should install and run on the versions in between. Only database versions that their vendor still maintains are actively supported, though. If you have a problem on a version that has reached end of life, the advice will be to move to a currently supported release. Check the MySQL and MariaDB websites for which versions those are. |
| PHP extensions | `pdo`, `pdo_mysql`, `session`, `json`, `mbstring`, `zip` | `zip` is needed for updates and content export. The installer asks for it up front so those features work later. |
| Web server | Apache with `mod_rewrite` and `.htaccess` files allowed | The shipped `.htaccess` files assume Apache. |
| Writable folders | `config/`, `storage/logs/`, `storage/cache/`, `storage/backups/`, `storage/sessions/`, `content/uploads/` | Must be writable by the web server user. |
| On the server | No SSH, Composer, or Node.js | Everything is installed and updated from the browser. |

The installer checks all of the above before doing anything else and shows a clear, specific message if something is missing.

## Installing

1. Create an empty database and a database user with full rights on it, using your host's control panel.
2. Upload the Lumora Fiction files to your web space, in the site's root folder or a subfolder.
3. Open the site in your browser. You are taken to the installer automatically; if not, go to `/install/` on your site.
4. Fill in the database details, the site name and address, and the first administrator account, then choose **Install Lumora Fiction**. You can also pick a starter list for ratings, warnings and relationship categories, or none; it is optional and can be done later under Settings.

The installer checks your server first and tells you plainly if something needs fixing, such as a missing PHP extension or a folder the web server cannot write to. It also tests that the database login can create and change tables before it builds anything. If something fails part-way, it removes what it created, so you can correct the problem and submit the form again.

When it finishes, the installer deletes its own folder. If your host does not allow that, it tells you to delete the `install` folder yourself; please do.

### Good to know

- **Table prefix.** The installer suggests a random prefix, such as `lumfic_a8f3d1_`, for the database tables. It keeps them apart from anything else in the same database and makes them harder to guess. Accept the suggestion unless you have a reason not to.
- **Login name and display name.** Your login name is used only to sign in. It is never public; you can see your own on your account page, and Administrators can see everyone's under **Users**. Your display name is the name readers see. They must be different.
- **Site address.** Links in emails, such as password resets, are built from the address you enter, so make sure it is the one people will really use.
- **Clean web addresses.** If the installer warns that they do not seem to work, ask your host to enable `mod_rewrite` and allow `.htaccess` files.

## Signing in

Open `/admin/` on your site and sign in with the login name (or email address) and password you chose during installation. Administrators, Editors, and Authors can use the admin area; Readers cannot.

## Accounts for your readers and writers

Visitors have their own pages at `/account/sign-in` and `/account/register`, linked from the site's main menu. Anyone with an account, whatever their role, can sign in there.

- **Registering.** The form asks for a private login name, a public display name, an email address and a password. If you ask for email confirmation under Settings > Archive, the new person is told to check their inbox, and the account starts working when they open the link. The page gives the same answer whether or not the address already had an account, so it cannot be used to find out who is registered. If registration is closed, the page says so and the menu link disappears.
- **Signing in and out.** Sign in with the login name or the email address. After five wrong passwords from one address, sign-in is paused for fifteen minutes, and the page says how long to wait. A person who has not yet confirmed their address is offered a new confirmation email. **Sign out** is a button on the account page.
- **Forgotten passwords.** **Forgot your password?** on the sign-in page emails a link that works once and expires after an hour. Opening the link does not use it up; only choosing a new password does. The page says nothing about whether the address has an account.
- **My account.** Signed-in people open **My account** in the menu to change their display name, email address and password. Changing the email address or the password needs the current password, and the old address is told about an email change. Changing the password signs the person out everywhere else. Authors also choose here how the chapter editor opens for them, or keep the site default; the choice is hidden while Settings > Editor locks everyone to one mode.

- **Dashboard.** Shows anything that needs attention, such as the installer folder still being on the server, and, for administrators, who has joined and what has changed recently.
- **Stories.** Where Authors write. **My stories** lists your own stories, searchable and filterable by status; Editors and Administrators also get **All stories**. Click a column heading (**Title**, **Author**, **Status**, **Chapters**, **Words** or **Last changed**) to sort the list, and click it again to reverse it; the arrow shows the column in use, and the search and status filter are kept. Tick several stories to publish, unpublish or delete them together; stories that are not yours, or that still lack the classification the administrator requires, are skipped and counted. **Add a story** opens the story form: title, summary, notes, disclaimer, language, where the story stands, an optional cover picture, and the classification lists the archive setting allows (a list marked "needed before publishing" must be filled in first). Fandoms, characters, relationships and tags are typed as a comma-separated list, and the entries that already exist and match what you are typing appear under the box: choose one with the arrow keys and Enter (or by clicking it) to use its exact spelling. An entry found through an alias is entered under its proper name, and one no administrator has reviewed yet is marked as new. Characters and relationships are narrowed to the fandoms you have already named. Without scripts the boxes still work as plain text. A cover is a JPEG, PNG or WebP picture of up to 2 MB (less if your host allows less), with a short description for readers who cannot see it; after you choose a picture, a frame in the shape the story page uses appears over it, and you can drag it or use the sliders to crop the picture (or leave it alone and use the whole picture); the site then resizes it and saves it again, so details in the file such as where a photo was taken are not kept. Covers need PHP's GD extension. Replacing or removing a cover, or deleting the story, removes the old picture files. Open a story to publish it, send it for approval, unpublish it, preview it, or manage its chapters. A story waits as a draft, and an Author's first story may wait for approval, depending on Settings > Archive; after one of their stories has been published, later ones publish directly. If a reviewer sends a story back, it returns to a draft with the reason shown on the story form and a **Not approved** mark in the list, until you send it for approval again. A chapter has a title, optional notes, and the editor with a live word count and separate **Save draft** and **Publish** buttons; **Move up** and **Move down** change the chapter order. A chapter is only visible to readers once both it and its story are published. **Preview** shows a draft the way it will read, to you and to staff only. Leaving a form with unsaved changes asks first, and if your session ends while you write, the page hands your text back so you can sign in again in another tab and carry on without losing it. Deleting asks on a page of its own and cannot be undone.
- **Author profile.** Authors, Editors and Administrators can tell readers about themselves. **About you** takes a short text, up to 10,000 characters, written in the same editors as a chapter (Visual, Markdown, HTML or plain text) with the same formatting, and **Picture** takes a JPEG, PNG or WebP picture (at least 100 pixels wide and tall, up to 2 MB, with the same optional crop as a story cover) that is resized to a 100 by 100 square. Both appear in an **About the author** section above the **Stories by** heading on your author page, and nothing is drawn until you fill something in. Everything here is public. The screen is also linked as **Author profile** under **Quick links** on your account page. Administrators can see both on a person's page under **Users** and use **Clear author information** or **Remove picture** (recorded in the activity log) if something should not be public.
- **Notifications.** Authors, Editors and Administrators have an inbox, under **Stories** and as **Notifications (N)** in the header when something is unread, for what needs them about stories. Reviewers are told when a story is waiting for review, and a writer is told when a reviewer approves their story, sends it back (the reason itself is on the story's form) or unpublishes it. Each message links to the page that deals with it, can be marked as read, and is marked as read by itself once the thing it asks for has happened. Nobody is told about what they did themselves. It is separate from **Alerts**, which are about the site and only for Administrators.
- **Trash.** **Move to the trash** on a story's page, on a chapter's page or from the bulk menu takes it out of every list and away from readers without deleting it. A story in the trash is unpublished; a chapter in the trash leaves the story, and the chapters after it move up. **Stories > Trash** lists what you have trashed (Editors and Administrators can also show everyone's), where you can **Restore** it, as a draft or unpublished and never straight to readers (a chapter comes back at the end of its story), or **Delete for good** after a confirmation page. What stays in the trash is deleted for good after the number of days set under Settings > Retention. **Delete the story** and **Delete this chapter** still delete at once, as before. Bookmarks and reading places that pointed at a trashed chapter are dropped.
- **Copying a story.** **Make a copy** on a story's page creates a new draft with the same details and classification and every chapter as a draft. It has no cover picture, and none of the readers' bookmarks. A copy is yours, even if you copied another writer's story as an Editor or Administrator.
- **Earlier versions of a chapter.** Each time a change replaces a chapter's title, text or notes, the version it had is kept. **Earlier versions** on the chapter's page lists them with who saved them, shows any of them as it reads, and **Go back to this version** puts it back; what it replaces is kept too, so going back can be undone. Only the newest versions are kept (Settings > Retention).
- **Awaiting approval.** Editors and Administrators see the stories waiting for review here, the one waiting longest first (click the **Title**, **Author**, **Chapters**, **Words** or **Sent** heading to sort by it instead), and the dashboard shows how many there are. **Preview** a story to read it the way it will appear, then **Approve** it to publish it, or **Send back** with a reason the author will read; a sent-back story becomes a draft again. Taking a published story down (**Unpublish** on its form) keeps the story, its chapters and its classification, and removes it from every public list at once. Every decision is recorded in the audit log with who made it.
- **Classification.** The lists writers choose from when they describe a story: fandoms, characters, relationships, genres, tags, categories (for original fiction), ratings, archive warnings, and relationship categories. Ratings and warnings are separate lists, and one is never worked out from the other. Writers can add new fandoms, characters, relationships, and tags while they write; these can be used at once and are marked **not yet reviewed** until you approve them. You can rename a term, add other names that lead to it (aliases), merge duplicates so every story moves to the term you keep, and delete terms nothing uses. Characters and relationships can belong to a fandom, so the same name in two fandoms stays two terms. Choose which lists a story must have before it is submitted; a list with nothing on it is never required, and is left off the story form and the search filters. The short lists (ratings, warnings, categories, genres and relationship categories) have a description for each value, shown to writers beside their choice, and can be put in the order you like with **Move up** and **Move down**; **Hidden** takes a value off the choices and the public indexes while stories that already have it keep it. The ready-made starter lists (see Settings > Archive) are optional ways to fill them. Lists that your archive setting hides (for example fandoms on an original-fiction site) are kept and come back if you switch the setting back.
- **Users.** Administrators see everyone on the site here, newest first, and can search by display name, login name or email address and filter by role and status. The list and each person's page show their login name, which only Administrators see. Click a column heading (**Name**, **Email address**, **Role**, **Status** or **Joined**) to sort by it, and click it again to reverse the order; the arrow shows the column in use, and the search and filters are kept. Open a person to see their role, status, when they joined, whether their email address is confirmed, how many stories they have in each state, and a link to their author page once they have published. From there an administrator can change their role, and for an Author choose how their stories are published: follow the site setting, publish without review, or have every story reviewed. **Suspend this account** asks first, signs the person out at once, stops them signing in or writing, and can also take all their published or waiting stories out of view (kept as drafts, and the page says how many); **Reinstate** lets them back in, and a person who never confirmed their email address goes back to waiting for that when the site requires it. You cannot change your own role or suspend yourself, and the site always keeps at least one active administrator. Each change can carry a reason and is recorded in the audit log with who made it.
- **Appearance.** **Themes** lists the themes installed on the site as cards with a preview picture, and lets an administrator choose the one the public side uses. **Details** opens a window with the theme's version, author, description and what it needs (it is also an ordinary section on the page when scripts are off). A theme can be added by uploading a ZIP of its folder under **Add a theme**, updated from a newer ZIP in its Details (its saved options and its place as the theme in use are kept), and deleted: tick one or more cards, choose **Delete**, and confirm; the theme in use and the built-in theme cannot be deleted, and a theme that comes with Lumora Fiction asks for a clear confirmation because the next update puts it back. A theme is program code that runs on your site, so only add themes you trust; a host that prefers to copy theme folders in by hand can switch uploading off with `'theme_uploads' => false` in `config/config.php`. Copying a theme's folder into the `content/themes/` folder of your site still works and shows up in the list at once. If the theme you chose is removed or stops working, the site keeps working with a plain built-in theme and the screen tells you, and putting the theme back restores it. **Theme options** shows the settings the chosen theme offers, such as colours and reading width; the bundled **Storybook** theme is the default and offers the story typeface and the footer credit, while readers choose light or dark colours, text size and reading width themselves, remembered in their browser; anything left on "use the theme's own" keeps the look its designer chose. Previews of draft stories and chapters are drawn by the chosen theme, so a preview is exactly what readers will see.
- **News.** Editors and Administrators write announcements for readers here: downtime, rule changes, events, new features. **Add a news post** takes a title and text in the same editors as a chapter (Visual, Markdown, HTML or plain text) and has **Save draft**, **Publish** and **Unpublish** buttons, a preview drawn by the chosen theme, and an unsaved-changes warning; deleting asks on a page of its own. **Pin to the top** keeps a post above newer ones. The list can be searched, filtered by draft or published, and sorted by clicking a column heading, and the dashboard shows how many posts there are. A post is not a story: it has no categories, tags, comments or schedule, and any Editor or Administrator can change any post. A post's address (`/news/12-its-title`) follows its title until the first time it is published and stays the same afterwards, even if it is unpublished and published again. Every change is recorded in the audit log.
- **Settings > Front page.** How many recently published and how many recently updated stories the home page lists (8 each by default, from 0 to 30; 0 leaves that list off the page). Whether anyone can register is still set under Settings > Archive.
- **Settings > News.** The switch **Show news on the site** and how many posts the home page shows (3 by default, from 0 to 20; 0 leaves news off the home page). Switching news off hides the News page, every post address, the News link in the menu and the home page block, and keeps all the posts for when it is turned back on. The admin News screens stay available.
- **Settings > Retention.** How many days a story or chapter stays in the trash before it is deleted for good (30 by default; 0 keeps them until someone deletes them), and how many earlier versions are kept for each chapter (20 by default, from 1 to 200). A long chapter's versions take room in the database, so a lower number saves space on a small host.
- **Settings > Archive.** Choose whether the site publishes fanfiction, original fiction, or both; whether anyone can register; what role new accounts receive; whether new accounts must confirm their email address; whether a new Author's first story waits for approval; and whether visitors must sign in to read. The recommended settings are the defaults, and the archive is open to read unless you change that. **Starter lists** adds ready-made values to a list so you do not have to type them: ratings in the AO3 style, the FanFiction.net style, a film-rating style or a minimal three-value set; archive warnings in the AO3 style or a minimal set; relationship categories (F/F, F/M, Gen, M/M, Multi, Other); and whether writers may mark a story abandoned. Each has a short description written for this site, and none is ever applied unless you choose it. Adding a list only adds what is missing, so it never removes or changes a value you renamed, hid, described or reordered, and adding it again only brings back values you deleted. If you would rather make your own values, leave a list empty and add them under Classification.

## Reading the archive

The home page lists the most recently published and the most recently updated stories, with the latest news in a column beside them (below them on a phone). Each story has a page at `/story/42-its-title` with its cover, summary, notes, details, tags and chapter list, and each chapter is read at `/story/42-its-title/1`, with the author's notes, previous and next links, and its place in the story. A finished story that has a single chapter shows its text on the story page itself. When you are signed in and may change a story (its author, an Editor or an Administrator), each chapter in its list also has an **Edit** link that opens the chapter in the admin; no one else sees it. Every author who has published something has a page at `/author/7-their-name`, and every fandom, character, relationship, genre, tag, category, rating, warning and relationship category has its own listing, for example `/fandom/good-omens` or `/tag/slow-burn`. Lists can be sorted by recent update, publication date, title or length, and are split into pages of twenty stories.

**News** is in the main menu, at `/news`, while news is switched on. It lists the published announcements ten to a page, pinned posts first and then the newest, and each post has its own page showing its title, date, the author's display name and the text. The latest few also appear at the top of the home page. News is open to everyone who can reach the site, including when the archive is set to require sign-in, so it can tell people about downtime or a rule change.

**Browse** is in the main menu, at `/browse`. It links three plain lists, **New stories**, **Recently updated stories** and **Completed stories**, and an index for each kind of classification that has something to show: fandoms, genres, categories, tags, ratings, archive warnings and relationship categories. An index lists each name with the number of stories that use it, A to Z or most used first, with a row of first letters (and `#` for names that start with a digit or a symbol) to jump to one, and is split into pages of a hundred. Only names an administrator has reviewed, and that at least one public story uses, are listed, so a new tag proposed by a writer or a tag that exists only on a draft does not appear until it qualifies (its own page still works). A kind that your archive mode switches off is not offered. Characters and relationships have no index of their own, because there are too many; a fandom's page links to its most used characters and relationships instead.

**Search** is in the main menu, at `/search`. Type words to find stories whose title, summary, author name or tags contain all of them, and open **Filters and sorting** to narrow the results by rating, archive warning, genre, category, fandom, tag, whether the story is ongoing or finished, its language, and a range of word counts; the kinds your archive mode switches off are not offered. Fandoms and tags are typed by name (an alias works too); the others are picked from a list. Results can be ordered by recent update, publication date, title or length. Every filter in use is shown above the results, and clicking it removes it. The page works without scripts, and its addresses can be bookmarked or shared. A search handles up to six words of 100 characters and shows at most the first thousand matches, split into pages of twenty, so a very broad search is better narrowed with a filter. Search pages are kept out of search engines.

Only stories that are published, and chapters that are published, are ever shown. A draft, a story waiting for approval, an unpublished story or a deleted one gives the same "page not found" as an address that never existed, so nobody can tell it is there; its author and the staff who can edit it are taken to the private preview instead. A listing for a tag or fandom with no public stories, and the page of an account that has published nothing, also answer "not found". If you turn on **Visitors must sign in to read** under Settings > Archive, anyone who is not signed in is sent to the sign-in page instead of seeing any story page.
- **Settings > Editor.** Choose the editor writers start with: Visual (a formatting toolbar), Markdown, HTML, or plain text. A chapter can be switched to another mode at any time without losing its formatting, and you can lock everyone to your choice. The editors are part of Lumora Fiction and need no internet connection. Text pasted from Google Docs or Word keeps its italics, bold, underline, links and lists in the Visual and Markdown editors (the Markdown editor receives the formatting as Markdown), and any HTML in a chapter gets the same treatment when it is saved. Tables are not supported, so a pasted table becomes one paragraph per cell. Pasting into the HTML or plain text box brings in only plain text, because that is all a browser hands to a text box.
- **Maintenance > System Information.** Shows what your server offers and how the site is set up. **Download a snapshot** produces a file you can send when asking for help. It leaves out passwords, the secret key, the database name and table prefix, and the location of folders on the server.
- **Maintenance > Plugins.** Plugins add features to the site. To install one, copy its folder into the `content/plugins/` folder of your site, then open this screen and press **Turn on**. Each plugin shows its version, author and description, and a plugin that needs a newer PHP or a newer Lumora Fiction than yours says so and cannot be turned on. A plugin is run once as you turn it on, and one that fails to start is refused with the reason. If a plugin that is already on later breaks (after an edit, or a PHP upgrade), the site carries on without it, the dashboard says which plugin was skipped and why, and the reason is also written to the error log; fix or remove the plugin, or press **Turn off**. Only install plugins from people you trust, because a plugin can do anything the site itself can.
- **Dummy Content plugin.** A bundled plugin for testing, off until you turn it on. Under **Maintenance > Dummy content** it makes the number of placeholder users, fandoms, tags, stories, chapters and bookmarks you ask for, so you can try themes, lists and search at the size of a real archive, and one button removes everything it made and nothing else. It refuses to load unless `config/config.php` of that copy of the site has `'environment' => 'development'` (or `'testing'`); the installer writes `'production'`, so a live site can never run it by accident. Do not set that on a site with real readers. Its [own README](content/plugins/dummy-content/README.md) has the details.
- **Settings > Spam protection.** Guards the registration form, and the password-reset and confirmation-email forms, against programs that fill them in. Every form carries fields only a program would fill in and a note of when it was shown: a form that fills those fields is dropped without a word, and a form sent within a few seconds of loading is sent back with a request to wait. Registration also has a shortest wait between two submissions from one internet address, a cap per address per hour, and a pause for the whole site when a flood of sign-ups arrives from many addresses (the dashboard says so, and you can open registration again from this page). Every submission counts, whether or not it was filled in correctly, and a limit of 0 turns that limit off. You can also refuse throw-away email services (on by default) and your own list of email domains, ask a question that people find easy and programs do not, and, if you choose, have new sign-ups checked by Akismet, an outside service that is off unless you enter a key and turn it on. Visitors who share one address, such as a school, share one allowance for the per-address limits; behind your own proxy or CDN, list it under Settings > Security so each visitor has their own.
- **Maintenance > Alerts.** One place, for Administrators only, for problems with the site itself. It gathers what used to be scattered notices (debug mode left on, the installer folder still on the server, the site not served over HTTPS, a storage or uploads folder that cannot be written to, a plugin that failed to load, registration paused after a flood) and adds alerts kept from events: a run of locked sign-ins or refused registrations in an hour, and email that the server could not send. Alerts are grouped by severity (critical, warning, notice), each with a plain message, how long it has been going, a **Fix this** link to the right screen and, for the kept ones, **Dismiss**. A dismissed alert stays hidden while the problem keeps happening and comes back if it returns after a quiet week; kept alerts are removed after 30 days. Problems that are checked live clear by themselves once fixed. The admin header shows **Alerts (N)** with the number of critical alerts and warnings, the dashboard shows the most serious few, and Editors and Authors see none of it. A switch on the screen, off until you turn it on, emails critical kept alerts to the oldest active Administrator account, at most once per alert per day.
- **Maintenance > Security events.** Lists what the protections blocked, newest first: sign-ins locked after too many failures, forms that looked automated, and registrations that were refused or dropped. It records the kind of event, the internet address, the time and a short note, and never what a visitor typed. Entries are removed after 30 days, which you can change under Settings > Spam protection.
- **Too many failed attempts.** After five wrong passwords from one address, sign-in is paused for fifteen minutes. **Settings > Security** changes the number, the length of the pause, and whether an address that keeps failing is paused for longer each time (on by default; each further batch of failures within a day doubles the pause, up to sixteen times). When a pause starts and the last sign-in named an account, the owner of that account gets an email saying so, at most once an hour; switch this off on the same screen. The sign-in page says the same thing whether or not that account exists.
- **Settings > Security: passwords, sign-in length and HTTPS.** The same screen sets the shortest password allowed (8 to 64 characters, usually 10; it applies to passwords set from then on, never to ones already set, and the sign-up, reset and password forms show the current number), how many hours a sign-in lasts without activity (1 to 720, usually 24; nobody is signed out when it changes), and an **HTTPS only** switch that tells browsers to refuse plain HTTP for the site for a year. Because that cannot easily be undone once browsers have seen it, it can only be switched on from a page opened over HTTPS, and it is off by default.
- **Settings > Security: sites behind a proxy or CDN.** If your site is served through a reverse proxy or a CDN such as Cloudflare, every visit seems to come from the proxy, so the pause above and the registration limits would treat all your visitors as one. List your proxies under **Trusted proxies** and the site reads the visitor's address, and whether they used HTTPS, from the headers the proxy adds. The screen shows the address your own visit came from and the address the site recorded, so you can see whether it works. List only proxies you control, and leave the box empty if you have none: the site believes those headers from anything on the list, and a list that is too wide lets a visitor choose the address that is recorded. Very wide ranges are refused.
- **Light and dark.** The admin area follows your device's setting, and the button in the header switches it by hand.
- **Writer links on the account page.** Authors, Editors and Administrators find **Post New Story** and **My stories** on their **My account** page, and Editors and Administrators also find **Awaiting approval**. Readers see none of them.
- **Bookmarks, continuing and reading preferences.** Anyone signed in can press **Bookmark this story** on a story page, or **Bookmark this chapter** on a chapter page, and open **My bookmarks** from the **My account** page to see the list and remove a bookmark. The site also remembers the chapter you opened last in each story and how far down it you got, so the story page offers **Continue reading**, a chapter offers **Continue where you left off**, and **Continue reading** on your account page lists every story you have started, marks the finished ones, and lets you forget one or clear the list. **Follow this story** on a story page (or the tick box when bookmarking) adds it to the stories the site watches for you, and **Updated stories** on your account page lists the ones with a new or revised chapter since you last read them; with **Tell me when a story I'm reading has been updated** switched on in the Reading section, the story page, the account page and the Continue reading page also say so. Following is always your choice, and **Stop tracking** or **Stop tracking all** ends it without touching your bookmarks or places. Nothing is recorded for visitors who are not signed in. A story that its author later takes down disappears from your bookmarks without any message, and returns if it is published again. The **Reading** section of your account page sets colours, text size and reading width, and these follow you to any device you sign in on; the Storybook theme's own controls on a chapter save to your account as well. Bookmarks, places and preferences are private to your account and are deleted with the stories they point to. Each account can keep up to 500 bookmarks and follow up to 500 stories.
- **Moving between the site and the admin.** While you are signed in as an Author, Editor or Administrator, the site's main menu shows an **Admin** link, and the admin header has a **View site** link back to the home page. Readers and visitors never see the Admin link.
